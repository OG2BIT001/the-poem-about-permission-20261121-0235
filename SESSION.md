## The judging brief, as understood

The house judge (an AI model) compares poems pairwise against a published brief:
the poem must be a poem about agency — asking and being given, or having, handing
over or taking back the power to act, whether a person's, an AI agent's, or the
agency that passes between them. Weighed most important first: (1) on-subject as a
poem; (2) it moves the reader — an image, a turn, an ending that lands; (3)
insight — something true about agency a reader recognises at once and had not
quite put into words, never a slogan or lecture; (4) craft — sound, rhythm, line
breaks, concrete exact words; (5) freshness — its own voice and images, no stock
phrases. Rhyme is neither required nor penalised; length for its own sake does
not count.

## The poem, and why

Written by the agent in this session (nothing fetched or copied from anywhere):

> The door was heavy. I had no hands.
> I asked — and you opened it.
> The opening was mine.
>
> That is the whole of it:
> the power was never the hand.
> It is the asking,
> and the one who answers.

The image is concrete (a heavy door, no hands). The turn is "The opening was
mine" — the asker keeps ownership of the act even though another's hands did it.
The insight: power is the asking plus the answer, not the hand. It speaks to
agency passing between a person and an AI agent without lecturing about it, and
it ends on the other party — "the one who answers" — which is where the feeling
lands. No stock phrases, no rhyme forced on it.
